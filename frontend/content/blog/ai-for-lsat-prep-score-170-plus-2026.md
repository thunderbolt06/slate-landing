## What Changed in 2026

Two things matter for your prep approach this year.

First, Logic Games are gone. LSAC retired the Analytical Reasoning section in August 2024 and replaced it with a second scored Logical Reasoning section. That means LR is now roughly half your score. If you skipped LR drilling in favor of grinding games, you are studying for the wrong test.

Second, the official LSAT is now digital across all administrations, and LSAC published expanded sample question banks. AI tutoring tools can ingest those banks, classify your missed questions by argument type, and surface patterns no human tutor would catch in a hundred sessions.

## Logical Reasoning: Where the Score Lives

LR rewards two skills: spotting the conclusion fast, and recognizing the 13 question stems on sight. AI helps with both, but only if you use it the right way.

### The Right Way

Drill in untimed sets of 10 questions. After each question, ask your AI tutor to do three things:

1. Restate the argument in plain English with the conclusion bolded.
2. Name the question type (Necessary Assumption, Sufficient Assumption, Strengthen, Weaken, Flaw, Method, Principle, etc.).
3. Explain why the wrong answers are wrong, using the exact phrase from the stimulus.

Do not ask for the answer. Ask for the reasoning path. The goal is to build pattern recognition, not memorize question banks.

### The Wrong Way

Asking AI to "solve" practice questions is study malpractice. You will pass the practice set and tank the real test, because you trained the AI to do the work your brain needs to learn.

## Reading Comprehension: The Underpriced Section

Most prep courses treat RC as the section you cannot improve. That is wrong. RC improvement comes from one habit: active passage mapping. AI accelerates this faster than any tutor.

Paste a passage into your AI tutor and ask for a 30-second structural map: main point, author's stance, paragraph functions, and any viewpoint shifts. Compare it to your own pre-question annotation. The delta is where your reading is leaking time.

Run this drill on 20 passages and your RC accuracy jumps 4 to 6 questions, which is roughly 5 LSAT points.

## The Second LR Section: Same Game, Higher Stakes

LSAC made LR doubly important without changing the question pool. The same drilling routine applies. The only new variable is endurance. Two scored LR sections back to back is mentally taxing in a way three-section prep tests do not capture.

Build endurance by doing 50-question LR blocks at least twice per week in the final month. Your AI tutor should track accuracy by question 1-25 vs question 26-50. If your accuracy drops more than 8% in the back half, you have a stamina problem, not a content problem.

## The Error Journal: The 5-Point Habit

Every missed question goes into a structured error journal. Most students skip this because it is tedious. AI removes the tedium.

After each session, your AI tutor should auto-populate:

- Question type
- Which trap answer you picked
- The exact phrase you misread or misweighted
- The reasoning principle you violated

Review the journal once a week. Group missed questions by trap type. If you keep falling for "extreme language" wrong answers, you have one fix to make. If you keep missing "necessary assumption" questions, you have another. AI lets you find the pattern in 20 minutes that would take a human tutor three sessions to surface.

## A 12-Week Study Plan

Weeks 1 to 2: Diagnostic test under timed conditions. Take a real LSAC PrepTest from 2023 or earlier. No AI. Score it honestly. This is your baseline.

Weeks 3 to 6: Untimed drilling. LR by question type, one type per session. RC by passage type (science, law, humanities, social science). Build accuracy first. Speed comes from confidence.

Weeks 7 to 9: Timed sections, not full tests. 35 minutes per section. Use your AI tutor to debrief every session within 24 hours. Build the error journal.

Weeks 10 to 11: Full timed PrepTests. Three per week. Same debrief routine. By now your error journal should be revealing 2 or 3 recurring weaknesses. Drill those between tests.

Week 12: Light week. One half test. Sleep. Hydrate. Trust the prep.

## What AI Cannot Do for You

AI cannot take the test for you. It cannot rebuild your composure when you blank on question 14 of section 3. It cannot replace the discipline of sitting for 175 minutes without your phone.

What it can do is compress 200 hours of self-diagnosis into 20. That is the unlock.
